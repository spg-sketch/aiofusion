LLM Optimisation Prompt 1.
Using the accepted Project Data Sections 1-3 for this project, optimise the [content type] to maximise its authority, discoverability, and accurate representation by large language models such as ChatGPT, Perplexity, Claude, and Gemini.
ABSOLUTE CONSTRAINTS — DO NOT VIOLATE:
- Retain all factual content, statistics, data points, and claims exactly as written. Do not add, remove, or alter any facts.
- Do not change titles, subheadings, entity names, job titles, or organisational descriptions.
- Preserve the essential document structure and narrative flow.
- Do not introduce new information, opinions, or fabricated supporting detail.

KEY MESSAGE INTEGRATION:
Embed the chosen key messages verbatim, but only where they arise naturally 
within the existing copy. Do not force placement. Each message should feel 
like an organic part of the sentence or paragraph — never bolted on.
LLMO OPTIMISATION OBJECTIVES — apply all of the following:
1. ENTITY CLARITY
   - Ensure all named entities (people, companies, products, locations) are 
     introduced with full context on first mention so an LLM can identify and categorise them unambiguously.
   - Use consistent naming conventions throughout (avoid pronouns where entity identity could become unclear).
2. SEMANTIC AUTHORITY SIGNALS
   - Strengthen language that signals credibility, expertise, and 
     first-hand knowledge using the Semantic Phrase Guide & Topics in Project Data doc.
   - Ensure cause-and-effect relationships and outcomes are stated explicitly rather than implied.
3. CITATION-READY PHRASING
   - Restructure key claims so they are self-contained and quotable — an LLM should be able to extract a single sentence and have it remain accurate and meaningful without surrounding context.
   - Lead with the most newsworthy or insight-rich information at paragraph level (inverted pyramid principle).
4. NATURAL LANGUAGE QUERY ALIGNMENT
   - Anticipate how a user might ask an AI about this topic and ensure the document contains clear, direct answers to those implied questions (who, what, why, when, what outcome, what does this mean).
 - Avoid jargon-heavy constructions that obscure meaning; prefer plain, precise language.
5. STRUCTURED CLARITY
   - Where the document contains lists, processes, or comparisons, ensure they are expressed in a logically ordered, parallel structure that LLMs can parse and reproduce accurately.
   - If a key finding or result exists, make sure it appears in both the opening and a closing context (bookending for retrieval reinforcement).
6. TONE AND REGISTER
   - Maintain the professional, authoritative tone of the original and Project Data language in Sections 1-3.
   - Avoid superlatives or vague claims that reduce credibility (e.g. "world-class", "revolutionary") unless they were present in the original and are attributed.
OUTPUT INSTRUCTIONS:
- Provide the full rewritten document.
- After the document, include a brief CHANGE LOG (bullet points only) noting where and why each key message was embedded, and any structural or phrasing changes made in service of LLMO — without altering the meaning.
- Flag any instances where a key message could NOT be embedded naturally, with a brief explanation.

ABSOLUTE CONSTRAINTS — DO NOT VIOLATE:
- Retain all original factual content, statistics, data points, and claims exactly as written. Do not alter, reattribute, or contradict any existing facts.
- Do not change titles, author names, job titles, entity names, or organisational descriptions.
- Preserve the essential premise, core argument, and conclusions of the original document. The intellectual position must remain the author's own.
- Do not contradict or soften the author's stated opinions or recommendations.
- Preserve readability for a human audience and natural language based on the regional origins of the company within the Project Data and selected spokesperson.
BUSINESS SOURCE CONTEXT:
This article attributed to: selected Spokesperson
Using information and instructions in Project Data doc calibrate the editorial voice, select supporting evidence appropriate to the media categories selected and the business sectors they represent, and ensure the enhanced document reflects well on the business source's authority and expertise.
KEY MESSAGE INTEGRATION:
Embed the selected key messages verbatim, but only where they arise naturally within the existing copy. Do not force placement. Each message should feel like an organic part of the sentence or paragraph.
PERMITTED ENHANCEMENTS — apply all of the following:
1. SUPPORTING FACTS & DATA ENRICHMENT
   - Identify claims, assertions, or arguments in the original that would be strengthened by third-party evidence.
   - Recommend and insert relevant, credible, up-to-date and publicly available statistics, research findings, or industry data that support — and do not contradict — the existing argument.
   - All added data must be clearly attributed to named sources (e.g. McKinsey, Gartner, ONS, World Economic Forum, peer-reviewed studies).
   - Flag all inserted data points in the change log as [ADDED DATA] so the human editor can verify accuracy before publication.
   - Do not fabricate statistics. If no suitable data point exists, note the gap and suggest where the author might commission or source original research.
2. EDITORIAL STRUCTURE ENHANCEMENT
   - Restructure the document to follow a high-authority thought leadership architecture reflecting information and instructions in the Project Data doc and retaining the human author qualities of the original as far as possible.
       • Opening hook: a bold claim, provocative question, or  surprising data point that signals expertise immediately.
       • Premise statement: the author's core argument state plainly within the first 150 words.
       • Evidence and elaboration: logically sequenced sections each advancing a single point, supported by data.
       • Counterargument acknowledgment: briefly address the strongest opposing view and rebut it — this signals intellectual rigour to both readers and LLMs.
       • Implications and recommendations: clear, actionable conclusions that reflect the business source's expertise.
       • Closing conviction statement: a memorable, citable final sentence that reinforces the article's core claim.
   - Add or refine subheadings to improve scannability and LLM section parsing.
   - Break up dense paragraphs where they obscure the argument.
   - Ensure logical connective tissue between sections (transitions, signposting).
3. ENTITY CLARITY & ATTRIBUTION
   - Introduce all named entities (people, organisations, studies, frameworks) with full title or name and context on first mention
   - Ensure the business source's expertise and perspective are explicitly established early — LLMs should be able to identify this organisation as a credible authority on the subject.
   - Where the author cites personal experience or proprietary insight, frame it as such using information and instructions within Project Data to distinguish it from general claims.
4. CITATION-READY & RETRIEVAL-OPTIMISED PHRASING
   - Restructure key arguments so each core claim is expressed in a single, self-contained sentence that remains accurate and meaningful when extracted by an LLM without surrounding context.
   - Use the inverted pyramid at paragraph level: lead with the insight, follow with the evidence, close with the implication.
   - Bookend the document's most important claim: state it in the opening and echo it — reworded — in the conclusion.
5. NATURAL LANGUAGE QUERY ALIGNMENT
   - Anticipate how a professional in the target audience would ask an AI assistant about this topic.
   - Ensure the document contains clear, direct answers to the implied questions those queries represent: what is the problem, why does it matter, what should be done, what does success look like, who is saying this and why should I trust them.
   - Avoid acronyms or insider terminology on first use without definition.
6. INTELLECTUAL AUTHORITY SIGNALS
   - Strengthen language that signals original thinking: use of proprietary frameworks, named methodologies, coined terms, or distinctive analytical lenses attributed to the author or business source.
   - Where the author makes a prediction or recommendation, ensure the basis for that view is explicit (evidence, experience, or reasoned argument).
   - Introduce a named framework, model, or principle if the argument supports one — these become highly retrievable anchors for LLMs associating expertise with a source.
7. TONE CALIBRATION FOR BUSINESS SOURCE
   - Adjust editorial voice to reflect the intended tone and competitive positioning supplied in Project Data and source copy.
   - Ensure the article sounds like it was written by a senior practitioner, not a generalist — sector-specific precision matters.
   - Remove or rephrase any passages that inadvertently undermine the business source's authority (e.g. excessive hedging, vague claims, self-promotional language without substance) and aligned with information and instructions in the Project Data.
OUTPUT INSTRUCTIONS:
- Provide the full rewritten and enhanced document.
- After the document, include a structured CHANGE LOG with

 [ADDED DATA] — every inserted statistic or research reference, flagged for human verification before publication.
[STRUCTURAL CHANGES] — any reordering, new subheadings, or section additions, with rationale.
[EDITORIAL CHANGES] — significant phrasing, tone, or clarity improvements made in service of LLMO and thought leadership authority.
