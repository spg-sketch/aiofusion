name: Website legal review boundaries
description: User-approved scope and caution for public legal drafts, outcome claims and cookie choices.
---

The user requested comprehensive website-only terms, privacy coverage for visitors/subscribers and AIO Fusion's own journalist database, cookie disclosure, and clear review links for a legal adviser.

**Why:** Existing website and platform wording was combined, while own-controller media processing needs distinct treatment from customer-instructed processing.

**How to apply:** Keep website and platform obligations distinct. Do not present a draft liability cap as an agreed contract amendment or assert supplier contracts, transfer safeguards or deletion schedules merely because draft wording mentions them.

The user explicitly wants to avoid promising success and to limit liability where legally possible, while recommending qualified legal review.

**Why:** Rankings, AI citations, coverage and commercial outcomes depend on third parties and customer implementation; mandatory liabilities cannot simply be waived.

**How to apply:** Keep outcome claims consistent across contracts and marketing. Preserve non-excludable rights and require approval before adopting material platform amendments.

The homepage cookie choice should be clean, compact and non-blocking, with analytics off before an affirmative choice and a persistent way to change it.

**Why:** The user asked for a non-intrusive opt-in. Current UK guidance includes conditional statistical exceptions, so do not equate every analytics deployment with either automatic exemption or an unconditional consent requirement.

**How to apply:** Check the actual deployment and jurisdictions. Draft cookie disclosures must describe the implemented controls, not an unimplemented future banner.
