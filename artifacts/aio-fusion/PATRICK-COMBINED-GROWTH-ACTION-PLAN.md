# Patrick's Combined SEO, Demo and Product-Priority Action Plan

**Prepared:** 27 August 2026  
**Source:** AIO Fusion demo question bank, 49 buyer questions  
**Builds on:** Task 158 and `SEO-GROWTH-PLAN.md`

## 1. Objective

Turn the buyer question bank into a repeatable commercial system that helps Patrick:

1. answer accurately and confidently in demos;
2. convert verified buyer questions into useful search content;
3. gather evidence about the objections that genuinely block sales; and
4. promote only validated needs into the product roadmap.

This is not a plan to build all ten suggested features. The PDF's ranking is a set of hypotheses. Demo evidence, product fit, commercial value and delivery effort should decide what is built.

## 2. Non-negotiable rules

- Keep ChatGPT and Claude as the supported product scope unless Patrick explicitly approves a strategy change.
- Never repeat a statistic, price, cost, retention period, security control, competitor claim or product capability without a current source.
- Describe planned capabilities as planned, not available.
- Use honest limitations as trust-building answers. Do not improvise a workaround in the room.
- Keep evidence and causation separate. A correlation between coverage and score movement must not be presented as proof that one caused the other.
- Preserve the Task 158 SEO architecture: central metadata, public route registry, pre-rendering, generated sitemap, visible breadcrumbs and crawlable links.
- Do not create several pages that compete for the same query. Each new page needs a distinct reader need and conversion path.
- Keep Search Console indexing and performance data marked as pending until owner access provides real figures.

### Known conflicts to correct

- The current product decision supports ChatGPT and Claude only. The suggested expansion to Perplexity, Gemini, AI Overviews and Copilot is not approved scope.
- The present team model is more detailed than the PDF's one-login or flat three-seat description. Agency account seats, project content seats, direct-client colleagues and managed-client restrictions must be described accurately.
- Agency-managed partner clients are managed workspaces and do not receive their own credentials. The PDF's statement that those clients can be invited to log in conflicts with the current access model.
- Current fair-usage rules must be taken from the running product and billing policy, not the PDF's 50, 75 and 150 tier claim.
- Claims such as "over 80% of LLM citations come from earned media" require a named, credible source or removal.
- Product costs such as "£3 per audit", "8p per article" and annual run-cost calculations require current measured evidence before use.
- "Append-only audit log" and data-residency language must match implemented controls and signed policy wording. Intent is not certification.

## 4. Patrick's pre-demo pack

Include:

### Deliverable B: three audience-specific demo routes

#### PR agency

Lead with:

1. multi-client workspace and project separation;
2. role and project access controls;
3. audit to action plan to content workflow;
4. client-ready reporting that is genuinely available;
5. agency service packaging and commercial use.

Ask during the demo:

- How many colleagues need account-level access?
- How many people work on each client?
- Is agency branding on exports a purchase condition?
- Where must results be shared today?
- What does the agency expect to sell each month?

#### In-house communications or marketing

Lead with:

1. plain-language recommendations;
2. audit to prioritised action to usable content;
3. competitor and trend evidence that is actually available;
4. effort required in week one and a normal month;
5. security and procurement readiness.

Ask during the demo:

- Who needs access and what must each person do?
- What evidence does the CMO require?
- Is an outside agency involved?
- Which incorrect AI claims about the brand cause concern?
- Would a limited trial change the buying decision?

#### SEO agency

Lead with:

1. technical GEO checks;
2. the distinction between presence monitoring and earned-media diagnosis;
3. transparent current methodology;
4. before-and-after content optimisation;
5. how outputs can enter the agency's reporting workflow.

Ask during the demo:

- Which engines and markets are mandatory?
- How many prompts do clients expect to see?
- Is a documented methodology a procurement requirement?
- Must scores enter Looker Studio or another dashboard?
- Does the agency have PR delivery capability or need a partner route?

### Deliverable C: honest limitation responses

Prepare short answers for:

- attribution is not yet causal;
- broader engine coverage is not currently supported;
- media monitoring and outreach distribution are not V1 capabilities;
- no unsupported locale or non-English capability;
- no promised integration, API, trial or white-labelling until it exists;
- earned-media effects may take longer than a monthly reporting cycle.

Each limitation answer should end with one discovery question, not an invented promise.

## 7. Evidence programme

Product evidence should be gathered before large roadmap commitments.

### Repeatability study

- Define a fixed protocol before running comparisons.
- Use the same project inputs and query set.
- Record model versions, timestamps and any environmental differences.
- Repeat across several brands and categories.
- Publish a pilot result as a pilot, not a universal benchmark.
- Use a stability band only if the observed data supports one.

### Time and effort study

Measure:

- time to complete a useful project intake;
- time to first audit result;
- time to move from result to approved action;
- typical monthly operator time;
- differences between agency and in-house users.

Do not quote "40 minutes" or any other setup time until measured.

### Coverage and outcome evidence

During beta:

- record dated coverage and content actions;
- record subsequent audit and diagnostic movement;
- preserve the underlying observations;
- label relationships as correlation unless causal evidence exists;
- ask permission before turning any result into a public case study.

### Demo evidence log

After every qualified demo, Patrick should record:

| Field | Example use |
|---|---|
| Buyer segment | Compare PR, in-house and SEO demand |
| Question or objection | Preserve the buyer's exact wording |
| Was it a blocker? | Separate curiosity from lost-deal risk |
| Current answer worked? | Improve the script |
| Requested capability | Feed the product evidence queue |
| Existing workaround | Estimate urgency |
| Commercial value | Note deal or expansion relevance |
| Next step | Trial, security review, proposal, follow-up or lost |

Review this log every two weeks.

## 8. Product-priority framework

### Priority 0: no-code commercial readiness

Complete first:

1. verified 49-question answer register;
2. one-page demo answer sheet;
3. three audience demo routes;
4. content ownership and commercial-use answer;
5. approved seat explanation;
6. procurement security summary;
7. agency partner economics;
8. agency GEO service blueprint.

### Priority 1: credibility and sharing

Assess next:

1. published methodology and repeatability evidence;
2. agency-branded or white-label export;
3. secure shareable read-only result or project view;
4. clearer client-ready reporting using current audit data.

These remove common purchase friction without changing the core measurement scope.

### Priority 2: workflow and proof

Validate through demos before committing:

1. read-only scores API for reporting tools;
2. coverage or release import;
3. clearly labelled score-correlation view;
4. misinformation flag and counter-brief;
5. external collaborator access if the existing project-seat model cannot meet the need.

Coverage import has high strategic value because it supports onboarding and evidence, but it is not a small feature. Data quality, matching and causation language must be designed first.

### Priority 3: market expansion experiments

Do not place into committed scope without repeated demand and an explicit strategy decision:

1. free single audit;
2. locale-scoped probing;
3. non-English support;
4. wider or higher-frequency probe tiers;
5. additional engines beyond ChatGPT and Claude;
6. workflow integrations with Slack, Teams, project tools or Drive.

The free-audit idea should be treated as a go-to-market experiment with abuse controls, cost limits and a defined conversion measure, not as a permanent free tier by default.

### Promotion rule

Promote a candidate when:

- several independent qualified buyers identify the same capability as a blocker;
- the request fits the target segment and product position;
- the expected commercial value is recorded;
- the current product cannot solve the need safely;
- delivery effort and operating cost are understood; and
- success can be measured after release.

A useful working threshold is three independent blocker signals from the same target segment, or one strategically important opportunity with explicit commercial value. Patrick can override the threshold, but the reason should be recorded.
